All three give the current step a stronger title, more space and a clear boundary from history. The screenshots use identical workflow data. Selected direction: option 3, current workspace with history rail.

Synthetic browser captures of proposed TUIs. Cyan identifies Senior; amber identifies Junior. The Active label, border and step marker also work without color.

1. Framed current turn

Keep history in chronological order, then give the active turn its own tinted frame. Its current step gets a bold, underlined heading. This adds the clearest separation with the smallest change to the timeline.

Tradeoff: a little vertical space goes to the frame. While browsing far back, keep a compact live pointer and an explicit return-to-current action.

2. Pinned current-work area

Put the active turn at the top and give it a fixed region. Previous turns live in a separate lower region. Current work remains the first thing you see while history updates.

Tradeoff: the screen no longer reads as one chronological transcript. Long outputs need scrolling inside the active region or an inspector.

3. Current workspace with history rail Selected

Move previous turns into a narrow navigation rail and devote the main workspace to the active turn. At 80 columns, the rail becomes a compact history control.

Tradeoff: reviewing older conversations takes more navigation, and the rail reduces the space available for long paths and commands.

Common behavior: selecting an older record never changes the Active label or the working agent. Current and past tools and agent runs remain inspectable. F1 opens help; Esc returns with the draft and read position intact.
